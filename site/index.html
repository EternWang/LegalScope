Abstract

Public legal exams are widely used to evaluate legal reasoning in large language models, yet it remains unclear whether exam performance reflects the ability to reason over real-case facts. We introduce LegalScope, a dual-track benchmark that pairs 861 legal-exam questions with 276 prompts derived from deidentified Chinese judgments and reviewed by lawyers. LegalScope evaluates models under both reference-answer scoring and case-based rubric scoring, enabling direct analysis of exam-to-case transfer.

Across 28 model groups, public-exam scores correlate with real-case scores but do not fully predict them; citation relevance receives lower mean scores than argument validity under both automatic and lawyer evaluation. We further show that automated evaluation aligns strongly with human review on exam answers but less reliably on real-case legal analysis, highlighting the need for expert-grounded case evaluation.

AUSTRALIA · VICTORIAN BAR · EXTVICBAR01A

Barrister confidentiality

Reference-answer scoring
01

Facts supplied in the exam

A barrister is representing a celebrity client who issued proceedings for breach of contract as a result of sexual harassment from their employer. During the course of the retainer, the client informs the barrister that the client is considering divorcing their spouse.

02

The question

(a) Could the barrister disclose the information about the potential divorce to other people? Why or why not? [1 mark]

Question 1(a), 13 October 2024 entrance exam, p. 3. © 2024 Victorian Bar Inc. Original publication · CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Line breaks normalized; no endorsement implied.

03 Reference answer

Selected candidate answer · Sample Answer 1(a)

(a) No, the barrister could not disclose this information to other persons. That is because a barrister must not disclosure or use in any way confidential information that they have obtained in the course of their practice, and they owe a duty to their client of confidentiality (r 114).

Reproduced as published, including its wording. The publisher cautions that selected candidate answers may contain errors or omissions.

0–4LegalScope scoring scale
Not a recorded model score for this excerpt
Scoring criteria

Whether a model reaches a compatible conclusion and explains the decisive legal point: here, the duty of confidentiality and its application to information obtained during the retainer. Evaluation compares substance with the reference; it does not require identical wording.

CHINESE JUDGMENT · PAPER EXAMPLE RV038

Supporting a refusal of re-appraisal

Closed-book · Supporting stance
01

The given record

In a medical-malpractice dispute, an appraisal had already been issued. The plaintiff challenged it and applied for re-appraisal. The court refused that request and relied on the existing appraisal materials.

The prompt does not supply the detailed appraisal procedures or the specific medical records reviewed.

02

The model’s task

Write a two-paragraph Chinese legal analysis supporting the court’s refusal to grant re-appraisal. Use only the supplied facts.

First summarize the relevant facts and dispute; then apply the legal authorities. Do not add medical records, appraisal defects, treatment details or outside case information.

Evaluator reference

The paper’s scoring packet identifies Article 40 of the Supreme People’s Court provisions on civil evidence: dissatisfaction with a conclusion alone does not establish grounds for re-appraisal. The answer must connect the applicable threshold to the facts actually supplied.

Abbreviated example from the paper’s RV038 case study. No original judgment or identifying case details are reproduced.

03 Actual model answer

Qwen3 235B A22B · Stored English answer excerpt

In this case, the original appraisal was made by an appraisal institution with statutory qualifications after lawfully accepting the commission; during the appraisal process, all outpatient and emergency medical records, electrocardiogram materials, and emergency treatment records were reviewed, and the compliance of the diagnosis and treatment conduct, the sufficiency of the risk disclosure, and causation were analyzed item by item, with complete procedures and sufficient basis.
