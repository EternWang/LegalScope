CHINESE JUDGMENT · PAPER EXAMPLE RV038

Supporting a refusal of re-appraisal

Closed-book · Supporting stance

1. Model input

2. Model answer

Qwen3 235B A22B · Full answer

In this case, the plaintiff alleged that the defendant medical institution failed to take sufficient measures while the patient was seeking treatment for acute chest pain, causing the patient to die because the patient was not transferred in time, and therefore filed suit seeking that it bear liability for compensation for medical malpractice, and applied for a medical malpractice appraisal. The appraisal institution has issued a formal appraisal opinion, finding that although the medical provider had made a transfer recommendation and attempted to coordinate with a higher-level hospital for admission, there were defects in the completeness of the risk disclosure in the medical record entries; therefore, the medical provider bore a certain degree of fault liability, but the causal relationship between that fault and the patient's eventual death was relatively low. The plaintiff objected to the appraisal conclusion's determination regarding the degree of causation, arguing that the appraisal opinion did not fully consider the boundary of responsibility of a primary-level hospital in identifying critical illness and making compulsory interventions, and applied for a new appraisal. The defendant medical institution argued that it had fulfilled its obligations of reasonable disclosure and assistance with transfer, that the patient's independent decision to leave the hospital was the key factor in the materialization of the risk, and that the original appraisal procedure was lawful and sufficiently supported, so the appraisal should not be restarted.

According to Article 79 of the Civil Procedure Law of the People's Republic of China and Article 40 of the Several Provisions of the Supreme People's Court on Evidence in Civil Proceedings, where a party objects to an appraisal opinion, the objection should first be resolved by applying for the appraiser to appear in court, conducting questioning, or submitting written cross-examination opinions, etc.; only where there are statutory grounds such as serious illegality in the appraisal procedure, manifest insufficiency of the appraisal basis, the appraiser's lack of qualifications, or circumstances requiring recusal may the court permit a new appraisal. In this case, the original appraisal was made by an appraisal institution with statutory qualifications after lawfully accepting the commission; during the appraisal process, all outpatient and emergency medical records, electrocardiogram materials, and emergency treatment records were reviewed, and the compliance of the diagnosis and treatment conduct, the sufficiency of the risk disclosure, and causation were analyzed item by item, with complete procedures and sufficient basis. The plaintiff's objection was, in substance, disagreement with the medical professional judgment in the appraisal conclusion, rather than pointing to obvious defects in the appraisal procedure or basis, and therefore did not meet the statutory conditions for a new appraisal. The court's refusal to permit a new appraisal was based on its reasonable acceptance of the probative force of the existing appraisal opinion, which both maintained the stability of judicial appraisal and prevented the litigation procedure from being improperly delayed by repeated appraisals, and was consistent with the value orientation and application logic of the current evidence rules.
